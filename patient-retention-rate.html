How to measure patient retention.

Most hospitals track revenue every day and retention never. These six numbers, reviewed monthly, show where patients are leaking and whether your fixes are working.

By Ramadhan Omar, Registered Nurse and Founder 6 min read Updated September 2026

In this guide

  1. Why measure retention
  2. The six numbers
  3. Calculating them from your register
  4. A worked example
  5. A 30-minute monthly routine

Why measure retention

Getting a new patient through the door costs marketing, reputation and time. Keeping a patient who already trusts you costs a reminder and a follow-up. Yet most facilities can tell you yesterday's collections and cannot tell you how many of last month's patients came back. You cannot manage a leak you never measure.

The six numbers

NumberFormulaWhat it tells you
Returning patient rateFirst-time patients from 90 days ago who returned at least once, divided by all first-time patients in that monthWhether your care and follow-up earn a second visit
No-show rateBooked appointments not attended, divided by all booked appointmentsHow many paid-for slots go empty
ANC completion rateMothers who delivered this month and completed the contacts due for their gestation, divided by all mothers who deliveredWhether mothers stay on the antenatal schedule
Immunisation on-time rateVaccine doses given within the due window, divided by doses dueWhether babies stay on the KEPI schedule
PNC follow-up reachMothers reached for postnatal follow-up, divided by mothers who deliveredWhether care continues after delivery
Patients slipping awayCount of patients with a follow-up due who have not returned in 60 or more daysA named list your team can act on this week

Calculating them from your register

You do not need software to start. From your register or a simple spreadsheet you need four columns per visit: patient ID, visit date, whether it was a first visit, and the next appointment date if one was booked. With those:

  • Filter first visits for a month three months ago, then check which of those IDs appear again later. That gives you the returning patient rate.
  • Count next-appointment dates that fell in the last month, then check how many of those patients actually came. The difference is your no-show rate.
  • Sort by last visit date. Anyone with a booked follow-up that is more than 60 days in the past goes on the slipping-away list.

A worked example

These are illustrative numbers, not results from a real facility. Picture a clinic that saw 200 first-time patients in June. By the end of September, 76 of them had come back at least once.

Returning patient rate = 76 / 200 = 38%. If the clinic lifts that to 45%, the same 200 first visits produce 14 more returning patients, every month, without spending anything on marketing.

Run the same arithmetic with your own numbers and your own average visit value. For most facilities, one extra returning patient a week pays for a follow-up system many times over.

A 30-minute monthly routine

  1. Calculate the six numbers on the first working day of the month.
  2. Compare each with last month. Circle anything that moved by more than five points.
  3. Hand the slipping-away list to a named person with a deadline.
  4. Pick one fix for the month, for example two reminders instead of one, or calling defaulters on Fridays.
  5. Check next month whether the number moved.
