The four PNC visits, and how to get mothers back.

Most mothers finish their postnatal care at discharge. Kenya's schedule has four visits over six months. Here is why mothers skip them and how private facilities bring them back.

By Ramadhan Omar, Registered Nurse and Founder 6 min read Updated September 2026

In this guide

  1. The four PNC visits
  2. Why mothers skip PNC
  3. Seven steps to better PNC completion
  4. PNC message templates

The four PNC visits

Kenya's Ministry of Health schedules postnatal care for mother and baby at four points:

VisitWhenWhat it catches
PNC 1Within 48 hours of birthBleeding, infection, blood pressure, early feeding problems, newborn danger signs
PNC 21 to 2 weeksWound healing, jaundice, weight gain, breastfeeding difficulties
PNC 34 to 6 weeksRecovery, family planning, mood and postnatal depression, baby's growth
PNC 44 to 6 monthsFamily planning follow-up, nutrition, mother's health

Summarised for planning follow-up. Clinical content should follow current Ministry of Health guidelines and the Mother and Child Health Handbook.

For mothers who deliver in your facility, PNC 1 usually happens before discharge. The visits that go missing are PNC 2, 3 and 4.

Why mothers skip PNC

  • "I feel fine." Once home, a mother focuses on the baby. Her own check-up feels optional.
  • Nobody booked it. Discharge notes often say "PNC in two weeks" with no date and no reminder.
  • She thinks the baby's clinic is enough. Many mothers do not know PNC is also for them.
  • Transport and cost, especially after a caesarean section.

Seven steps to better PNC completion

  1. Book PNC 2 and PNC 3 at discharge, with dates written in her handbook.
  2. Pair PNC 3 with the 6-week vaccines. The baby's first vaccines fall at 6 weeks, inside the PNC 3 window. One trip, two services, and a mother who is already coming for the baby.
  3. Explain that PNC is for her too, in one sentence: "At six weeks we check that you have healed well and talk about family planning."
  4. Teach three danger signs before discharge, and give her a number to message any time.
  5. Send a day-3 check-in message. "How are you and baby doing?" builds the relationship and surfaces problems early.
  6. Remind before each visit in her language, two days before and on the morning.
  7. Trace missed PNC visits the same way as missed vaccines. See the defaulter tracing system.

PNC message templates

Day-3 check-in

English

Hello {Name}, this is {Clinic}. How are you and {Baby's name} doing since you went home? If you have heavy bleeding, fever, or baby is not feeding well, please call us or come in immediately. Reply here if you have any question.

Kiswahili

Habari {Jina}, huu ni ujumbe kutoka {Kliniki}. Wewe na {Jina la mtoto} mnaendeleaje tangu mrudi nyumbani? Ukitokwa na damu nyingi, ukiwa na homa, au mtoto hanyonyi vizuri, tafadhali tupigie simu au uje mara moja. Jibu hapa kama una swali lolote.

PNC 3 with the 6-week vaccines

English

Hello {Name}, {Baby's name}'s 6-week vaccines are due on {Date} at {Clinic}. We will also do your own 6-week postnatal check on the same visit. Reply 1 to confirm or 2 to choose another day.

Kiswahili

Habari {Jina}, chanjo za wiki sita za {Jina la mtoto} ni tarehe {Tarehe} katika {Kliniki}. Siku hiyo hiyo tutakufanyia pia wewe uchunguzi wa baada ya kujifungua wa wiki sita. Jibu 1 kuthibitisha au 2 kuchagua siku nyingine.

How to measure PNC completion

PNC 3 completion = mothers who delivered with you and attended the 4 to 6 week visit ÷ all mothers who delivered with you, counted for mothers whose 6-week date fell last month. Track PNC 2 the same way. If PNC 3 completion is far below your 6-week vaccine attendance, the pairing in step 2 is your quickest win.
